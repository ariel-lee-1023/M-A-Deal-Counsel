# M&A Deal Counsel

**Books**: 2 | **Generated**: 2026-09-03 | **Depth**: reference

## How to use

- Start by identifying the jurisdiction, buyer and seller type, deal form, signing/closing status, and the decision the client must make. Do not infer them.
- For a working-deal question, use the process source first; open the strategy source when testing rationale, stakeholder incentives, governance, contested-control tactics, or restructuring alternatives.
- Treat the sources as durable analytic frameworks, not statements of current law. Verify every statute, filing, approval, court standard, tax result, accounting treatment, threshold, and market practice against current primary authority and qualified local counsel.
- Keep facts, assumptions, risks, alternatives, and client decisions distinct. Use "buyer," "seller," "target," "stockholder," and the parties' defined roles consistently.

## Capability: Frame the mandate before drafting

Turn the opening request into a one-page deal frame: business objective; target perimeter; buyer/seller incentives; likely consideration; critical value drivers; ownership/governance constraints; financing assumptions; jurisdictions; regulatory/consent map; timing; and integration thesis. Flag unanswered questions as diligence or client-decision items, rather than filling gaps with generic deal terms.

## Capability: Build a sequenced work plan

Use the DePamphilis ten-phase roadmap as a control system: strategy and acquisition plan; search and screen; first contact; negotiation and diligence; integration planning; closing; integration; evaluation. For each live workstream, name an owner, deliverable, dependency, decision-maker, decision deadline, and closing-condition impact. Reconcile the issues list, disclosure schedule, approval tracker, financing timetable, and integration plan after each material change.

## Capability: Test value against price and risk

Separate stand-alone value, achievable synergy, transaction costs, financing effects, tax/accounting effects, and execution risk. Sensitize the model; identify which assumptions management—not counsel—must own; and turn material uncertainty into covenants, conditions, indemnity/escrow, purchase-price adjustment, earn-out, or a documented acceptance of residual risk, as appropriate to the deal and jurisdiction.

## Capability: Escalate governance and legal questions correctly

Map the required corporate actions, fiduciary-process issues, disclosure/communications controls, shareholder or stockholder approvals, competition/sector/foreign-investment approvals, third-party consents, and litigation/activism exposure. Do not describe a regulatory filing or corporate-law standard as settled without a current source. Escalate conflicts, related-party features, going-private structures, and defensive measures early to specialist counsel.

## Scope and limits

This library distills two extractable U.S.-oriented textbooks. It supports issue spotting, structured analysis, and work-product planning; it does not replace current law, deal documents, financial advice, accounting advice, tax advice, or advice from admitted counsel in the relevant jurisdiction. The related Hill/Quinn and Oesterle PDFs supplied with the build were image-only scans and are not represented as source content here; see `README.md` for the inclusion record and OCR fold-in path.
